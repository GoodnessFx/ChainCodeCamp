| [Sherlock](https://www.sherlock.xyz) | Competitive audit (fixed payouts) | Fixed per valid finding | More predictable income |

Start with Code4rena or Codehawks. Both have lower-stakes contests that are good for learning the submission process.

## How Code4rena Works

1. A protocol pays C4 to run a competitive audit (typical prize pool: $20k–$200k)
2. The contest runs for 3–10 days — the repo is public, anyone audits
3. Wardens submit findings via C4's web interface
4. C4 judges review, deduplicate, and assign severity/validity
5. Payouts distributed proportionally to valid findings

**Severity → payout formula:**
- High findings: highest payout per valid unique finding
- Medium: moderate payout
- Low/Informational: small fixed amounts or nothing depending on contest
- Duplicate findings: the best-written duplicate usually gets more credit

**What "unique" means:** If 50 wardens find the same reentrancy bug, you split the High allocation 50 ways. Finding bugs fewer people found = higher payout. Focus on complexity, not obviousness.

## How Sherlock Works

Sherlock uses a fixed pricing model and has stricter validity standards than C4. Judges are highly experienced and reject vague or low-quality reports aggressively.

**The key differences:**
- You must prove impact with a PoC (no PoC = automatic rejection)
- Severity definitions are stricter — "users might lose funds in edge case" ≠ High
- You can escalate disputed decisions (and often should)

## What Separates Winning Reports from Noise

After reading hundreds of past contest reports, the pattern is clear:

**Reports that win:**
- State the impact in dollar terms or percentage of funds at risk
- Include a Foundry PoC with clear before/after assertions
- Explain the root cause, not just the symptom
- Suggest a specific fix, not "add validation"

**Reports that get rejected:**
- "This could potentially maybe cause issues if..." — no quantified impact
- No PoC, or a PoC that doesn't compile
- Duplicate of a higher-quality report on the same issue
- Informational issues submitted as Mediums hoping to sneak through

## Your First 90 Days: A Practical Path

**Month 1 — Read, don't submit:**
- Pick one contest that's already finished
- Read the final report
- Now try to find those same bugs in the original code
- Compare what you found vs what was found

**Month 2 — Shadow audit:**
- Find a live contest with fewer than 5 days remaining
- Audit it seriously, write your findings as if you're submitting
- After the contest closes, compare your findings to the public report

**Month 3 — Submit:**
- Pick a contest where you feel confident in at least 2 findings
- Submit properly-formatted reports with PoCs
- Expect your first submissions to be rejected or downgraded — that's normal
- Read the judge's rationale and adjust

## Immunefi: Bug Bounties on Live Protocols

Immunefi hosts bounties from live protocols — finding a bug here means real deployed contracts, real user funds at risk, and potentially 50k–10M USD in payout.

**How to approach it:**

1. Pick a protocol you understand from Section 06 (DeFi architecture)
2. Read their Immunefi scope carefully — out-of-scope submissions waste everyone's time
3. Audit the live code (Etherscan verified sources + GitHub)
4. If you find something critical: report it, don't exploit it
5. Give the team 72 hours to respond before any public disclosure

**The responsible disclosure rule is not optional.** Exploiting a bug you find rather than reporting it is theft. It also ends your career in public security.

## Tracking Your Progress

Keep a personal log for every contest you participate in:

```markdown
## Contest: ProtocolX (Code4rena, Jan 2025)

**Time spent:** 40 hours  
**Findings submitted:** 3  
**Results:** 1 Valid Medium, 2 Duplicates  
**Payout:** $340  

**Bugs I missed that others found:**
- H-01: Flash loan governance attack — I identified the governor had no delay
  but didn't chain it with the flash loan vector. Need to think more about
  composability.

**What I'll do differently:**
- For every governance contract: immediately test flash loan voting attack
```

This log becomes your portfolio. After 10 contests, patterns in your blind spots emerge. Fix them systematically.

## The Business Side

The income picture for a skilled auditor (18–24 months in):

- Competitive audits: $2k–$20k/month depending on contest selection and win rate
- Private audits: $5k–$50k per engagement (need 3–5 public wins to get hired)
- Bug bounties: Irregular, potentially life-changing single findings
- Full-time at a firm (Spearbit, Trail of Bits, Cyfrin, OpenZeppelin): $120k–$300k+

The fastest path to private audits: win something notable on a well-known protocol. One High finding on a top-100 DeFi protocol is worth more for reputation than 20 Low findings on small contests.
